# Primary-source evidence table

Checked 2026-10-22 through connected Firecrawl. Targeted reading, not a systematic review. Original summaries only; no paper text or figures redistributed. Four papers below were not the classics already cited in the preceding replay motif memo.

| Source and verified date | Evidence inspected | Supported claim | Design consequence and boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schapiro, McDevitt, Rogers, Mednick & Norman, **Human hippocampal replay during rest prioritizes weakly learned information and predicts memory performance**, Nature Communications 9:3920, published **2018-09-25**. [Publisher](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-06213-1), DOI 10.1038/s41467-018-06213-1. | Original open article: abstract, introduction and Methods participants/stimuli/procedure; publisher date metadata. 24 participants; item-level fMRI analysis of 15 learned satellite objects. | Weaker prior memory related to more subsequent rest reactivation; replay in the second session predicted subsequent memory. Across-session improvement related to replay only in the sleep group. | Motivate a weakness-based control. This is observational item-level neural/behavioral evidence, not a randomized manipulation of a priority rule. fMRI reactivation is not a direct recording of millisecond ripple sequences. Supervised cross-entropy is our analogy, not a measured human variable. |
| Mattar & Daw, **Prioritized memory access explains planning and hippocampal replay**, Nature Neuroscience 21:1609–1617, online **2018-10-22**, November 2018 issue. [Publisher](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-018-0232-z), [indexed original abstract/date](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30349103/), [author code](https://github.com/marcelomattar/PrioritizedReplay). | Publisher abstract and bibliographic/source metadata. Full text was subscription-limited; no claim of full-method inspection. | A normative spatial-navigation simulation orders memory access by prospective utility, balancing need and gain, and accounts for several replay phenomena. | Separates useful selection from indiscriminate rehearsal. A classification loss-increase rule is neither their gain-times-need calculation nor a reproduction of their planning model. No reward or future visitation distribution is modeled here. |
| Aljundi et al., **Online Continual Learning with Maximally Interfered Retrieval**, NeurIPS **2019**; arXiv first **2019-08-11**, inspected v3 **2019-10-29**. [Versioned abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04742v3), [full Methods §3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1908.04742v3#S3.SS1), [author code](https://github.com/optimass/Maximally_Interfered_Retrieval). | Abstract, version history, original full-text introduction and Methods §§3–3.1. | MIR computes a virtual incoming-batch update, then ranks memory examples by increased loss. The paper describes reservoir storage, candidate subsampling, and an alternative score requiring stored historical best losses. | Use the first score only, explicitly crediting MIR. Our class-quota matching and compute controls are study choices, not claims about the original benchmark. Avoid hidden best-loss state and count virtual gradients/candidate evaluations. Report no algorithmic novelty. |
| Schaul, Quan, Antonoglou & Silver, **Prioritized Experience Replay**, ICLR **2016**; arXiv first **2015-11-18**, inspected current v4 **2016-02-25**. [Versioned primary abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05952v4). | Primary abstract and submission/publication history. Full algorithm not audited in this pass. | Replay prioritization was already a useful ML approach in DQN/Atari experiments, rather than an invention of this proposal. | A second historical ML precedent. Our deterministic high-cross-entropy selector is NOT a reproduction of PER, its temporal-difference scoring, stochastic sampling or bias correction. We do not transfer its reported performance to supervised image classification. |
